The strips under `theo-body/` are frames of the story `face-visit` and of `face-in-shell` in the same headless Chromium, with the throw made by pointer events dispatched in the page, since that page is too slow for a real-time gesture to throw anything. They show where the figure goes, not how the motion feels. `theo-body/tuning.png` is the panel under Settings, Appearance, where the feel is set, in the dark theme, scrolled to its last rows with two settings changed. The three `theo-body/meet-*.png` strips are five frames each of one throw at Kit on `face-in-shell`, with that story's WebGL backdrop left out so the page keeps its frame rate and the page's clock turned one frame at a time between pictures. Its layout follows Twenty's settings as the rest of Settings does (rows in a box, the part a change is tried on at the top of it); where a slider, its value and a reset sit in a row was read from other products' settings screens on Mobbin (Greptile, Hume AI, PlanetScale, Krea AI, Shop, Dialpad, Linktree, beehiiv). Only the arrangement was taken: no picture, colour, type or wording of theirs is in the page or in this repository.
- Pointing has no collage: it was built to a sentence, not a picture. The six renders under `theo-pointing/` are the story `face-point` in headless Chromium on software WebGL at 2x, Theo sent by `perform` called in the page and the tap made by a real pointer click. They show where Theo stands, what is marked, and what a tap opens; they do not show whether a person reads the mark, and no agent sent Theo there during a live turn.
- Sound has no collage: the owner asked for a retro boombox and named no picture, and none is committed. The seven renders under `theo-sound/` are the stories `face-sound` and `face-in-shell` in headless Chromium on software WebGL at 2x. In six the sound is a tone the capture script makes, shaped like speech; in `reading.png` it is the Phonon voice reading the story's reply, its model fetched from Hugging Face by that browser. They show what Theo becomes while sound plays and that it is clear of the words; they do not show how the voice sounds, how the form moves, or whether a person reads it as a loudspeaker.
